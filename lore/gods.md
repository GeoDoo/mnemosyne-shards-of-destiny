# Gods & Divine Forces

> The divine forces in this world are not characters to be fought — they are cosmic principles that shape fate itself.

---

## The Three Pillars

### Mnemosyne — Keeper of Memory

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Domain** | Memory, cosmic order, the shape of reality |
| **Symbols** | The Well, The Mirror, The Thread |
| **Role** | Source of the Shards; created Alkmaeon as a vessel |
| **Appearance** | Never directly seen — manifests through visions, water reflections |

**Nature**: Mnemosyne is not a goddess who speaks or intervenes directly. She IS memory itself. When Alkmaeon experiences visions, he is touching her domain. The Wells scattered across Greece are her sacred places.

**In-Game Presence**:
- Visions and flashbacks
- The Well of Mnemosyne (Chapter 2)
- Memory-based abilities
- The final revelation (Alkmaeon is her creation)

---

### Hecate — Guardian of Thresholds

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Domain** | Crossroads, boundaries, transitions |
| **Symbols** | The Torch, The Key, The Triple Form |
| **Role** | Guide who appears at turning points |
| **Appearance** | Veiled woman bearing a torch; sometimes triple-formed |

**Nature**: Hecate walks the spaces between — life and death, mortal and divine, past and future. She does not command; she illuminates choices. Her guidance is cryptic but never malicious.

**In-Game Presence**:
- Appears at key story moments
- Never fights alongside or against the party
- Offers warnings, not solutions
- Final crossroads choice (Chapter 6)

**Dialogue Style**: Speaks in riddles, questions, and omens. Never gives direct answers.

---

### Apollo — God of Light and Prophecy

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Domain** | Light, music, prophecy, harmony, healing |
| **Symbols** | The Lyre, The Sun, The Temple |
| **Role** | His ruined temple is the story's frame |
| **Appearance** | Not seen directly; felt through his abandoned sanctuaries |

**Nature**: Apollo represents the order the gods once maintained. His temples are ruined because the divine attention has shifted elsewhere — mortals are left with only echoes of his light.

**In-Game Presence**:
- The Temple of Apollo (Chapters 1 & 7)
- Light-based imagery in Memory of Light vision
- His absence is felt more than his presence

---

## The Antagonist

### Typhon — The Buried Storm

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Domain** | Chaos, entropy, primordial destruction |
| **Symbols** | The Storm, The Serpent, The Earthquake |
| **Role** | The threat that must be contained |
| **Appearance** | Never fully seen — felt through tremors, storms, corruption |

**Nature**: Typhon is not evil in the human sense. He is chaos itself — the force that existed before order, and will exist after it crumbles. He does not hate; he simply IS.

**The Buried One**: Zeus defeated Typhon in the Titanomachy and buried him beneath the earth. But chaos does not die — it only sleeps. When the Order of Destiny weakens, he stirs.

**In-Game Presence**:
- Earthquakes and storms increase as story progresses
- Corrupted enemies spawned by his influence
- Memory of Ruin shows his perspective
- Never fought directly (he is too vast)

## Lesser Divine Figures

### Demeter — Goddess of Harvest
- **Connection**: Theano is her priestess
- **Role**: Background presence; her oracles sensed the disturbance

### The Muses — Daughters of Mnemosyne
- **Connection**: May appear in visions
- **Role**: Reinforce memory/art theme

### Charon — Ferryman of the Dead
- **Potential**: May appear if story goes to the underworld
- **Role**: Would be neutral, transactional

---

## Divine Rules

1. **Gods do not fight mortals** — They work through agents, visions, and influence
2. **No god is purely good or evil** — They are forces with their own logic
3. **Divine intervention is rare** — Mortals must act; gods only watch
4. **Memory is sacred** — Forgetting is death before death
